# Candidates

Scores are subjective, from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), in the judge's priority
order: (1) a dad joke a father would tell at the table, (2) pun lands cleanly
on the first read, (3) fresh, not told a thousand times, inside the dad joke
tradition, (4) tight, punchline at the end, (5) reads naturally aloud. These
are editorial estimates, not judge results.

| Candidate | Dad-table | Pun lands | Fresh | Tight | Aloud |

| 1. I told my kids the vacuum cleaner quit its job. It said the work sucked, the pay was dirt, and the boss kept pushing it around. | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 |

Candidate 1: a dad telling his kids, three vacuum-true puns stacked in one
sentence (sucked / dirt / pushing it around), the last one the punchline.
The single line "the work sucked" is a known vacuum gag, but the triple-stack
composition is original, and it is the only candidate that scores 5 on the
pun, the tightness and the aloud rhythm at once.

Candidate 2 has the freshest single pun ("toner of voice") but a weaker setup
(printers do not "look down") and only one payoff. Candidate 3 is clean but
"days are numbered" is a standard idiom dressed as a joke. Candidate 4 is a
verbal turn, not a pun — it would lose criterion 2 against a real pun.
Candidates 5–7 each carry one thin pun and a setup longer than the payoff.

Pick: candidate 1. It packs three puns into 137 characters, so against the
one-pun entries that fill this field it wins on density as well as on the
groan: every clause is a joke, and the last clause is the punchline.

One final refinement: "I told my kids" keeps the dad voice and the table
scene; "our vacuum cleaner" would add a word for nothing. No word cut is
possible without losing a pun — "It said" is the hinge the three puns hang
on. Final text unchanged from candidate 1.

Final: I told my kids the vacuum cleaner quit its job. It said the work sucked, the pay was dirt, and the boss kept pushing it around.
